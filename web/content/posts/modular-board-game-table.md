#### Thumbscrew Upgrade

The modular connectors utilize 5/16-18 bolts to fasten to the aluminum extrusion. You can upgrade the bolts that the [Modular Connectors](#modular-connectors) use to thumbscrews to allow these to be used tool-free.

McMaster-Carr sells a variety of these and you can select the ones that fit your style and budget: https://www.mcmaster.com/products/thumb-screws/thumb-screws-2~/thread-size~5-16-18/

### Roll-In Connectors

8020 sells a collection of roll-in connectors that can be quickly tiled into a T-Slot channel and will lock in place. This makes it easy to add additional components to the table that require no bolts to attach and can be quickly added or removed from the table.

They can be used vertically to attach panels to the top of the table or attached to the side of the table to allow [cupholders](#cupholders), [dice trays](#dice-trays) or anything else you'd to add.

#### Cupholders

Both 8020 and TNutz sell nylon cupholders that can be attached to the [Roll-In Connectors](#roll-in-connectors). You can combine these to make easily adjustable cupholders to hold your drinks away from the table so you're less likely to spill drinks onto your games or tabletop.

Here's a few Cupholder options:

- TNutz: https://www.tnutz.com/product/ac-015-f/

### Channel Guards and Crumb Protection

8020 sells T-Slot Cover's you can use to cover unused T-Slot. This can help prevent extra debris and crumbs from getting into the channels which can be pretty difficult to clean.

The economy covers are plastic and can be cut to size to fit your components as needed. https://8020.net/2823.html

Or if you'd prefer a more premium option they offer custom sized aluminum covers that they will cut to size and should fit cleanly into your extrusions. These are a *significantly* more expensive option. https://8020.net/2119-black-fb.html

The pieces I've linked are in black. But you can buy these covers in a bunch of different color options if you'd like to add color accents to your table.

### Extrusion Attached Power Strip

This adds a power strip to the bottom of one of your tables with a bunch of outlets to charge phones, laptops or tablets during a D&D game. If you're using the table as a worktop it provides plenty of options for tools and more to connect.

A few options are available and you can modify home power strips to attach to your table as well. My table uses the outlet's sold by McMaster-Carr: https://www.mcmaster.com/catalog/131/2246/3136N933

If you are adding LED lights to your table, this can also be an option to easily power those lights.

## Making All This a Bit Cheaper

You can reduce the cost of this table by removing the black anodization from most of the components. Many aluminum extrusion sellers sell black components at a higher cost than the silver options. If you make the table in silver it will be cheaper.

Additionally some components may be able to be swapped out for the light versions which use thinner and lighter-weight pieces. These are typically cheaper - however may have less structural strength. If those components fit your table will depend on what you intend to use it for, but if you can use them they will reduce the cost further.
